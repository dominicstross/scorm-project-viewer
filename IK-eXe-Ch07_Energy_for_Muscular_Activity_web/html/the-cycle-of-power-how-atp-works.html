The Cycle of Power – How ATP Works


To maintain homeostasis and continue performing mechanical work, the body must engage in a continuous cycle of energy exchange known as the ATP-ADP Cycle. Because muscle cells only store a very limited amount of ATP—enough for only a few seconds of movement—this cycle is the strategic lifeline of physical activity.

Phase 1: Releasing Energy (Hydrolysis) 

When the body requires energy for muscle contraction, it breaks the final phosphate bond in the ATP molecule. This chemical process is called Hydrolysis.

  • Reaction: ATP -->ADP + P
  • In this reaction, energy is liberated for work, and the molecule becomes Adenosine Diphosphate (ADP) and a lone inorganic phosphate (P).
  • Metaphorically, the "charged battery" is now "used" or "drained."


Phase 2: Regenerating Energy (Resynthesis) 

To continue working, the body must "recharge" the ADP back into ATP almost immediately. This process is called Resynthesis.

  • Reaction: ADP + P -->ATP
  • Unlike hydrolysis, resynthesis requires an input of energy. This energy is derived from the metabolic breakdown of food via our three distinct energy systems.


By constantly cycling between ATP and ADP, the body can provide a steady stream of power for mechanical work despite having minimal stores of actual ATP. This cycle ensures that energy "liberation" and energy "recharge" are perfectly balanced during exercise.
