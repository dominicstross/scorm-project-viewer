The Journey of Exertion – Three Systems Overview

The Journey of Exertion – Three Systems Overview

As physical activity begins and continues, the body does not rely on a single energy source. Instead, it strategically shifts its primary energy source based on the duration and intensity of the effort. This "Journey of Exertion" involves three systems working toward a singular goal: the resynthesis of ATP.

The Metabolic Timeline

The Phosphagen System (Immediate Energy):

  • Duration: 0–10 seconds.
  • Metaphor: A Rocket Launch.
  • Provides the instant, explosive power needed for a sudden burst of activity.


The Glycolytic System (Short-Term Energy):

  • Duration: 10 seconds to ~2 minutes.
  • Metaphor: A High-Octane Engine.
  • Takes over as the initial burst fades, powering sustained high-intensity effort.


The Oxidative System (Long-Term Energy):

  • Duration: >2 minutes.
  • Metaphor: An Efficient Power Plant.
  • The primary source for long-duration activities, endurance, and basic survival.
     

These systems are not mutually exclusive; they overlap throughout the journey to ensure there is never a gap in energy delivery. As one system reaches its capacity, another is already ascending to take the lead.

Study Questions

An athlete performs a maximal 5-second sprint. Which system is the primary provider, and what is its metaphor?

The Phosphagen system; A Rocket Launch.


Why is the Oxidative system compared to an "Efficient Power Plant"? 

Because it provides sustainable, long-term energy with a high capacity but lower power output.


How long does the Glycolytic system typically provide short-term energy? 

From 10 seconds to approximately 2 minutes.


What is the singular goal shared by all three energy systems? 

To regenerate (resynthesize) ATP.


Which system becomes the primary source after 2 minutes of sustained exertion?

The Oxidative system.


Why does the body shift its primary energy source as duration increases? 

Because the immediate and short-term systems have limited capacities and eventually run out of fuel.


What is the "Immediate Energy" system?

The Phosphagen system.


Describe the "High-Octane Engine" in terms of its timeline.

It is the Glycolytic system, dominant from 10 seconds to 2 minutes.
 

Does the body use only one system at a time?

No; the systems overlap as the body shifts the primary source. 
 

Which system is dominant during a 30-minute jog?

The Oxidative system.
