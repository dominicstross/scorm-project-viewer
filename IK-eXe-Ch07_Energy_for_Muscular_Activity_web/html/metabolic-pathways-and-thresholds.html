Metabolic Pathways and Thresholds

Metabolism is the sum of all chemical reactions required to maintain life and produce energy. These reactions are categorized by whether they occur with oxygen (Aerobic) or without it (Anaerobic). A critical strategic concept for kinesiology professionals is the Anaerobic Threshold—the point during increasing exercise intensity where the body can no longer meet energy demands through aerobic pathways alone and must shift toward anaerobic dominance.

The body manages its energy economy through two primary types of pathways:

Breakdown Processes (Catabolic)

These pathways release energy by breaking down complex molecules:

  • Lipolysis: Breaking down the FAT Store into Free Fatty Acids (FFA).
  • Glycogenolysis: Breaking down Glycogen into Glucose.
  • Protein Breakdown: Breaking down Body Protein into Amino Acids.

 

Synthesis Processes (Anabolic)

These pathways store energy or build tissue:

  • Lipogenesis: Creating Fat Stores from the FFA pool.
  • Glycogenesis: Creating Glycogen from the Glucose pool.
  • Protein Synthesis: Creating Body Protein from Amino Acids.


The Glucose Pool serves as the critical central hub of this system. Because the brain and high-intensity muscular work are heavily dependent on glucose, the body maintains a "fail-safe" process called Gluconeogenesis. This pathway allows the body to generate "new" glucose from non-carbohydrate sources (like amino acids or fats) when carbohydrate intake is insufficient. This ensures the Glucose Pool remains stable even during prolonged exercise or fasting, preventing metabolic collapse.

Ultimately, energy production is a dynamic chemical balance where catabolic breakdown and anabolic synthesis are constantly adjusted to meet the mechanical demands placed upon the body.

Study Questions

Define the difference between Catabolic and Anabolic processes. 

Catabolic processes involve the breakdown of molecules to release energy, while Anabolic processes involve the synthesis of molecules for storage or structure.


What is the "Anaerobic Threshold"? 

The exercise intensity at which the body transitions from primarily aerobic to primarily anaerobic energy production.


Explain the function of Gluconeogenesis as a physiological "fail-safe." 

It allows the body to create glucose from non-carbohydrate sources to maintain the Glucose Pool when stores are low.


Contrast Glycogenesis and Glycogenolysis. 

Glycogenesis is the anabolic synthesis of glycogen for storage, while glycogenolysis is the catabolic breakdown of glycogen into glucose for energy.


What occurs during Lipolysis? 

Stored fat is broken down into Free Fatty Acids to be used in the metabolic pool.


Why is the Glucose Pool considered the "central hub" of metabolism? 

Because it can be replenished by food, glycogenolysis, or gluconeogenesis to fuel critical functions and high-intensity work.


Does "Aerobic" metabolism require the presence of oxygen? 

Yes.


What is the byproduct of Protein Breakdown? 

Amino Acids.
 

Describe the process of Lipogenesis. 

It is the anabolic synthesis of fat stores from the FFA pool.
 

Analyze the metabolic consequence of exercising above the Anaerobic Threshold.

The body relies more on the Glycolytic system, leading to faster depletion of glycogen stores and an accumulation of metabolic byproducts.
