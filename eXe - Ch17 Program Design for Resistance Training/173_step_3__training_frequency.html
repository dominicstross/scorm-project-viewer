17.3 Step 3 Training Frequency

Introduction

Determining the optimal training frequency is a crucial aspect of designing an effective resistance training program. Training frequency refers to the number of training sessions completed within a specific timeframe, usually a week. Strength and conditioning professionals must carefully consider various factors, including the athlete's training status, the demands of their sport season, the intensity of their workouts, the types of exercises being performed, and any other concurrent training activities. By understanding how these factors interact, professionals can create a training schedule that maximizes performance while minimizing the risk of overtraining.


The balance between training and recovery is paramount. Adequate rest is essential for muscle repair and adaptation, but insufficient training frequency can lead to a decline in fitness levels. This section will explore the key considerations for establishing an appropriate training frequency, including guidelines for rest intervals, strategies for advanced athletes, and the impact of training load and exercise type. Understanding these principles will enable professionals to develop individualized training programs that optimize athlete performance and promote long-term development.

Training Frequency

The amount of times an athlete trains in a week, known as training frequency, is a key part of resistance training. Professionals should look at the athlete's fitness level, sport season, workout intensity, exercise types, and other training activities. How ready an athlete is for training impacts how many rest days they need. Generally, three weekly workouts are recommended, but more advanced athletes can do more. Rest days should be between one and three days between sessions targeting the same muscles.


Training too infrequently can reduce fitness, but highly trained athletes can maintain strength with one weekly session. Advanced athletes might use split routines, training different muscle groups on different days. For example, upper and lower body can be trained on separate days. In-season sport training may reduce weight room time, affecting training frequency.


Heavier loads need more recovery. Upper body recovers faster than lower body, and single-joint exercises recover faster than multi-joint exercises. Powerlifters, for instance, might only do one heavy deadlift weekly. Overall physical stress from other activities can also reduce resistance training frequency.

Summary

Training frequency, or the number of weekly training sessions, is a vital component of resistance training programs. Professionals must consider the athlete's training status, sport season, exercise load, exercise type, and other activities. Beginner athletes typically benefit from three weekly sessions with appropriate rest intervals. Advanced athletes can utilize split routines, to increase training frequency by targeting different muscle groups on separate days.


The demands of the sport season, especially during the in-season, often necessitate a reduction in resistance training frequency. Training load and exercise type also play a significant role in recovery time, with heavier loads and multi-joint exercises requiring more rest. Additionally, concurrent training and other physically demanding activities can impact the overall stress load on the athlete, necessitating adjustments to resistance training frequency.

True-False

Question 1

Training frequency is determined by the athlete's favorite type of music.

Feedback

False

Training frequency is determined by factors such as training status, sport season, exercise loads, exercise type, and other activities.

Question 2

Beginners should train the same muscle groups every day.

Feedback

False

Beginners should include at least one rest or recovery day, but not more than three, between sessions that stress the same muscle groups.

Question 3

Split routines involve training all muscle groups in every session.

Feedback

False

Split routines involve training different muscle groups on different days.

Question 4

During the in-season, resistance training frequency should be increased.

Feedback

False

During the in-season, resistance training frequency is typically decreased.

Question 5

Lighter loads require more recovery time than maximal loads.

Feedback

False

Maximal loads require more recovery time than lighter loads.

Question 6

Multijoint exercises allow for faster recovery than single-joint exercises.

Feedback

False

Single-joint exercises allow for faster recovery than multijoint exercises.

Question 7

Concurrent training has no effect on resistance training frequency.

Feedback

False

Concurrent training may require a reduction in resistance training frequency.

Question 8

Training sessions should never be spaced out evenly for beginners.

Feedback

False

Training sessions should be spaced out evenly for beginners to prevent a decrease in training status.

Question 9

Lower body muscles recover faster than upper body muscles.

Feedback

False

Upper body muscles recover faster than lower body muscles.

Question 10

All athletes should train 7 days a week.

Feedback

False

Rest days are required for proper recovery, and training frequency is based on many variables, not just maximum possible days.
