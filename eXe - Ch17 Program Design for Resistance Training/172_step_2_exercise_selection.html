17.2 Step 2 Exercise Selection

Introduction

Effective resistance training programs hinge on the strategic selection of exercises. This process requires a comprehensive understanding of various exercise types and their relevance to specific sport movements. Strength and conditioning professionals must consider factors such as the athlete's training experience, available equipment, and time constraints. By analyzing the sport's demands and the athlete's capabilities, appropriate core and assistance exercises can be chosen to enhance performance and minimize injury risk.
The selection of exercises goes beyond simply choosing movements. It involves understanding the interplay between structural and power exercises, ensuring muscle balance, and incorporating recovery exercises. The specificity concept, or SAID principle, underscores the importance of mirroring sport-specific movements in training. Ultimately, informed exercise selection is crucial for maximizing the benefits of resistance training and achieving athletes' performance goals.

Exercise Selection

When designing a resistance training program, exercise selection is crucial. This process requires understanding different exercise categories and their sport relevance. Core exercises, which involve large muscle groups and multiple joints, are prioritized. Assistance exercises target smaller muscles and single joints. Structural exercises load the spine, and power exercises are performed explosively. Movement analysis ensures exercises align with the sport's demands, and the SAID principle emphasizes sport-specific training. Maintaining muscle balance between opposing muscle groups is vital for injury prevention. Recovery exercises promote restoration.
An athlete's training status and technique influence exercise choice. Professionals should instruct athletes on correct technique, especially for complex movements. Equipment availability and time constraints also dictate exercise selection. Limited equipment may require alternative exercises, and time limitations necessitate prioritizing efficient movements.

Summary

Exercise selection is a critical component of resistance training program design. It involves choosing appropriate exercises based on various factors, including the type of exercise, movement analysis of the sport, athlete's technique experience, equipment availability, and training time. Core exercises, which recruit large muscle groups and involve multiple joints, are prioritized for their direct application to sport performance. Assistance exercises target smaller muscle groups and single joints, often used for injury prevention and rehabilitation. Structural exercises load the spine, and power exercises are performed explosively.
The specificity concept (SAID principle) emphasizes the importance of selecting exercises that closely mimic sport-specific movements. Maintaining muscle balance between opposing muscle groups and incorporating recovery exercises are also essential for injury prevention and restoration. Strength and conditioning professionals must consider the athlete's training status, equipment availability, and time constraints when selecting exercises to ensure an effective and efficient training program.
