17.4 Step 4 Exercise Order

Introduction

The arrangement of exercises within a resistance training session, known as exercise order, plays a critical role in optimizing performance and preventing fatigue. By strategically sequencing exercises, athletes can maintain proper technique and maximize their force capabilities throughout the workout. This ensures that high-skill, high-demand exercises are performed when the athlete is freshest, while less demanding exercises can be placed later in the session.

This section will delve into the various methods of arranging resistance exercises, including prioritizing power exercises, alternating upper and lower body or push and pull movements, and utilizing supersets and compound sets. Each method offers unique benefits and considerations, allowing strength and conditioning professionals to tailor exercise order to meet the specific needs and goals of their athletes.

Exercise Order

Exercise order is the sequence of resistance exercises in a training session, aimed at maintaining maximal force with proper technique. Power exercises, like the snatch, should be done first due to their high skill and fatigue demand, followed by core and then assistance exercises, which can also be described as multi-joint then single joint, or large muscle groups then small muscle groups.

Alternating upper and lower body exercises allows for better recovery and reduces rest time, especially useful for untrained individuals and for circuit training. Alternating "push" and "pull" exercises also aids recovery by preventing consecutive use of the same muscle groups, and is also used in circuit training.

Supersets involve opposing muscle groups, while compound sets target the same muscle group, both being time-efficient but demanding. These methods may not be suitable for unconditioned athletes.

Summary

Exercise order is the strategic sequencing of resistance exercises to optimize performance and prevent fatigue. Power exercises, requiring high skill and concentration, are typically performed first, followed by core and assistance exercises. Alternating upper and lower body or "push" and "pull" exercises enhances recovery and efficiency, particularly in circuit training.

Supersets and compound sets, which involve pairing exercises for opposing or same muscle groups, respectively, offer time efficiency but are more demanding and may not be suitable for unconditioned athletes. The core goal of exercise order is to maintain proper technique and maximize force capabilities throughout the training session.
