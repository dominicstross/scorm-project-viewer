17.6 Step 6 Volume

Introduction

Volume is a critical component of resistance training, directly influencing the outcomes of strength, power, hypertrophy, and muscular endurance. It encompasses the total amount of work performed during a training session, quantified through metrics like repetition-volume and volume-load. Understanding how to manipulate volume, including the number of sets and repetitions, is essential for designing effective training programs. The application of these concepts extends beyond traditional resistance exercise, finding relevance in various athletic activities such as running, plyometrics, and swimming.


The choice between single and multiple sets, the athlete's training status, and the primary resistance training goal all play significant roles in determining optimal training volume. Historically, researchers have explored various set and repetition schemes, leading to current guidelines that emphasize tailored approaches based on individual needs and objectives. This section of the text delves into the nuances of volume manipulation, providing insights into how to maximize training adaptations and achieve desired performance outcomes.

Volume

In resistance training, volume is the total workload, measured by repetition-volume or volume-load, which is sets multiplied by reps and weight. This relates to concentric work and intensity. Single sets suit beginners, while advanced athletes need multiple sets. Training status dictates volume progression. Volume is also tied to training goals. For strength, sets of 6 or fewer reps are optimal; power uses lower volume for quality. Hypertrophy requires high volume, 6-12 reps, and 3-6 sets. Muscular endurance uses 12+ reps, lighter loads, and fewer sets. Fatigue impacts later sets. Running intensity relates to volume-load equivalents via metabolic energy cost.

Summary

Training volume, defined as the total work performed, is a fundamental aspect of resistance training. Volume-load, calculated by sets, repetitions, and weight, is directly related to concentric work and intensity. The effectiveness of single versus multiple sets depends on the athlete's training status, with beginners benefiting from single sets and advanced athletes requiring multiple sets for continued gains. Training volume should be progressively increased as athletes adapt.


The primary resistance training goal dictates optimal volume. For strength, sets of six or fewer repetitions are recommended, while power training utilizes lower volumes to maximize exercise quality. Hypertrophy requires higher volumes, including moderate to high repetitions and multiple sets. Muscular endurance programs focus on high repetitions with lighter loads and fewer sets. Fatigue is a factor to consider when prescribing multiple sets.

Feedback

Question 2

Feedback

False

Question 3

Feedback

False

Single-set training is most suitable for untrained individuals, while multiple-set training is best for advanced athletes.

Question 4

Training volume should decrease as an athlete gains experience.

Feedback

False

Training volume should increase as an athlete gains experience.

Question 5

12 or more repetitions is the optimal range for maximizing strength.

Feedback

False

6 or fewer repetitions is the optimal range for maximizing strength.

Question 6

Power training volume is higher than strength training volume.

Feedback

False

Power training volume is lower than strength training volume.

Question 7

Low volume, high intensity is the most effective for hypertrophy.

Feedback

False

High volume, moderate to high repetitions is the most effective for hypertrophy.

Question 8

Muscular endurance training uses low repetitions and heavy loads.

Feedback

False

Muscular endurance training uses high repetitions and lighter loads.

Question 9

Fatigue increases the amount of repetitions that can be achieved.

Feedback

False

Fatigue reduces the amount of repetitions that can be achieved.

Question 10

Running speed is comparable to volume load.

Feedback

False

Total metabolic energy cost is comparable to volume load.
